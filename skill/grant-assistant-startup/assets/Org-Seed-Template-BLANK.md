# Grant Assistant — Organization Seed Template (BLANK)

**Template version:** v0.1
**What this is:** the one document your organization fills out to stand up its Grant Assistant. When it's complete, you attach it to a Claude thread with your Airtable connected and run the startup skill once. The skill reads this template, builds your Airtable base, loads your existing grants, and generates your four operating skills.

**How to fill it out:**
- Under each field is an *instruction* (what to write) and an *example* drawn from a real-style organization, so you can see the shape of a good answer. Delete the guidance and replace it with your own content, or answer inline — either works.
- Fields marked **★ REQUIRED** must be filled. The system halts if any is blank, because these are the fields your skills actually reason from. Everything else improves quality but won't stop the install.
- You don't need perfect prose. Clear, honest, specific answers beat polished vague ones. If something isn't true yet, say so plainly — the system is built to flag gaps, not to have you invent things.
- Two example organizations appear throughout: **CRAI** (a ~6-month-old rural-AI nonprofit) and **FCAS** (a 15-year animal shelter). They're deliberately different so you can see how the same field works for a young org and a mature one.

### A5. ★ REQUIRED — Stage posture (what you may and may not claim)
*Instruction:* This is one of the most important fields. State honestly what evidence you can and cannot claim in proposals, based on your maturity. ## SECTION H — CURRENT PROJECTS & GRANT PORTFOLIO

> **This section seeds your Airtable and is your deduplication baseline.** Every grant you list here becomes one row in your **Grant Master** table — the single table that holds every grant at every lifecycle stage. A **Stage** field on each row (Possible / Active / Awarded / Archived / Withdrawn) tells the system what's in play, so your scans won't re-surface grants you already hold or already rejected. Grants you're actively working also get a linked working row in your Active Grants view; awarded grants get a Portfolio row. For recurring grants, note the cycle — the system tracks when they reopen.

### H1. Active / in-progress grants
*Instruction:* For each: funder, program, amount, status, and **cycle** (one-time / annual / every N years / rolling) with the next-open date if recurring.
*Example:*
- "PetSmart Charities — Spay/Neuter Access — $60,000 — Active, Year 2 of 3 — **every 3 years**, renews 2027
- La Plata County Animal Services Contract — $120,000/yr — Active — **annual**, renews each January
- Colorado Dept. of Agriculture Animal Welfare Grant — $45,000 — Active — **annual competitive**, reapply each spring"

> _Your answer:_

### H2. Prospective / pipeline (identified, not yet applied)
*Instruction:* Grants you've spotted but haven't pursued. These become your starting Grant Master records at Stage = Possible.
*Example:* "ASPCA equine/large-animal grants (targeted for the new equine program); USDA Rural Development facility funding (eligibility unconfirmed)."

> _Your answer:_

### H3. Awarded / completed / historical (optional)
*Instruction:* Past grants worth remembering, especially recurring funders you've won before. These load at Stage = Awarded or Archived so they're part of your dedup memory.
*Example:* "Multiple prior-year PetSmart and Colorado Dept. of Ag awards (10+ year history); one-time 2019 capital grant for building expansion (closed)."

> _Your answer:_

### H4. Funder / donor list (from your CRM or spreadsheet)
*Instruction:* Your known funders and donor prospects — current, past, and identified. Name and type (foundation / government / corporate / individual-major / civic) per funder; a website helps. Paste from your CRM export if you have one. These become your **Donor Master** records; funders of your active grants also get an Active Donors relationship row.
*Example:* "PetSmart Charities (national foundation, current funder); Colorado Dept. of Agriculture (state government, current); Yampa Valley Community Foundation (community foundation, prospect); Chewy corporate giving (corporate, past inquiry)."

> _Your answer:_

### H5. Funder restrictions (optional but important)
*Instruction:* Any funder you are currently restricted from approaching, and why: a decline with a wait-period, an org-wide exclusion, a program-specific limit, or a relationship-required rule. Include the end date if there is one. These seed your **Donor Restriction Log**, which the scan reads so it never surfaces a restricted funder.
*Example:* "Gates Family Foundation — declined 2025, asked us not to reapply before 2027 (post-decline window, until 2027-03). Local hospital foundation — funds only healthcare delivery orgs (org-wide)."

> _Your answer:_

### I3. ★ REQUIRED — Award bands (strong-fit grant size)
*Instruction:* The grant size range that's a *strong* fit for you right now. ## SECTION M — FUNDING PRIORITIES & FIT RULES (your judgment layer)

> **This section defines how your Grant Assistant decides what's worth pursuing.** It's fully yours to set — the examples are one org's answers, not defaults you must accept. These fields drive your scoring skill directly.

### M1. ★ REQUIRED — Funding priority order
*Instruction:* Rank the *types* of funding you want, highest to lowest. Types: unrestricted/general operating, capacity-building, program/project-restricted, capital, loan/repayable. Put what your organization most needs at the top.
*Example:*
1. "General operating / unrestricted (sustains our core)
2. Program-restricted for the equine expansion (strategic priority)
3. Capital (land, barn, fencing — high priority for us right now)
4. Recurring program grants (spay/neuter, disaster — protect the base)
5. Loans — case-by-case for facility build-out only"

> _Your answer:_

### M2. ★ REQUIRED — Fit criteria and what "strong" looks like
*Instruction:* The four things that make a grant a good fit for you, each with a description of what a *strong* (vs. weak) match looks like. The system scores each 1/3/5. Common criteria: mission alignment, geographic/regional relevance, program relevance, funding-size fit — but define yours.
*Example:*
- "Mission alignment — strong = directly about animal welfare/sheltering/rescue; weak = unrelated cause.
- Regional relevance — strong = names Four Corners / rural CO as a priority; weak = excludes our area.
- Program relevance — strong = maps to an active program (sheltering, spay/neuter, disaster); moderate = maps to the equine program being scoped.
- Funding size — strong = within our award band; weak = far outside it (unless capital exception)."

> _Your answer:_

### M3. Readiness criteria (optional but recommended)
*Instruction:* What determines whether you can *actually apply right now*, separate from whether a grant fits. Note any capacity limits.
*Example:* "Eligibility fit; deadline feasibility; partner requirements (CSU/county/vets usually sufficient); whether an existing program maps (equine = still scoping); application burden — with only 4 staff, a large federal application is heavy even when we're eligible."

> _Your answer:_

### M4. ★ REQUIRED — Durable rules (your non-negotiables)
*Instruction:* The rules your Grant Assistant must never violate, one per line. These are the honesty guardrails that protect your credibility. Include anything you never want to happen in pursuit of a grant.
*Example:*
1. "Don't claim equine-program outcomes that don't exist yet.
2. Protect the companion-animal core — expansion funding must not cannibalize the base mission.
3. A grant we currently hold is not a new opportunity; respect recurring cycles.
4. Emotional appeals stay honest and non-graphic."

> _Your answer:_

### M5. ★ REQUIRED — Loan posture
*Instruction:* How should the system treat loans and repayable capital? Choose one: **Exclude outright** (never recommend loans as grants) or **Case-by-case — flag for board** (a loan may be a legitimate decision; flag it for human review rather than rejecting).
*Example:* "Case-by-case — flag for board (as a mature org with audited financials, a facility loan for the build-out may be a legitimate board decision)."
*Example (young org):* "Exclude outright — we're not positioned to take on repayable capital."

> _Your answer:_

### M6. ★ REQUIRED — Recurring eligibility traps
*Instruction:* The eligibility gotcha(s) that repeatedly catch your organization — the thing a grant might look aligned on but disqualify you for. Every org has at least one. The system checks this on every opportunity so it doesn't score something high that you're actually ineligible for.
*Example (animal shelter):* "Species scope — many companion-animal funders exclude livestock/equine. Confirm species eligibility before scoring an equine application high."
*Example (fiscally sponsored org):* "Many funders require a direct 501(c)(3) and exclude fiscally sponsored organizations. Confirm fiscal-sponsor eligibility before investing in an application."

> _Your answer:_

### M7. Other eligibility nuances (optional)
*Instruction:* Any other standing eligibility notes — award caps as a % of budget, procurement-vs-grant distinctions, geographic locks.
*Example:* "Government animal-services money is often a contract, not a grant — track separately. Some funders cap awards at ~10% of operating budget."

> _Your answer:_

### M8. Partner sufficiency (optional but recommended)
*Instruction:* When a grant requires a partner, who do you already have that counts as sufficient? Prevents the system from flagging partner requirements you can already meet.
*Example:* "CSU Extension, La Plata County, or a regional veterinary clinic is generally a sufficient partner."

Confirm the ★ REQUIRED fields are all filled: A5 stage posture, D1 search geography, E1 programs with maturity, I1 budget, I3 award bands, M1 funding priority order, M2 fit criteria, M4 durable rules, M5 loan posture, M6 recurring eligibility traps.
